Project Write-Up

Rationale

Our goal was to make it easy to explore how temperatures across California could change over time under different greenhouse gas emissions scenarios. We chose an interactive geographic heatmap because it allows users to see not only how temperatures change over time, but also how those changes vary across different parts of the state.

We represent annual average temperature using color. Each grid cell is colored according to its temperature, with lighter yellow colors representing lower temperatures and darker orange and red colors representing higher temperatures. This encoding makes spatial temperature differences easy to recognize at a glance. We also included a color legend.

We chose a geographic map rather than a traditional line chart because our main goal was to show the spatial distribution of temperature across California. A line chart would be useful for comparing temperature trends over time, but it would not communicate geographic differences as directly.

We included two main interactive controls: an emissions scenario dropdown and a year slider. The dropdown allows users to switch between the low-emissions SSP1-2.6 and high-emissions SSP5-8.5 scenarios. The slider lets users explore temperatures from the historical period through 2100. When either control changes, the map updates to display the corresponding temperatures. Users can also hover over or tap individual grid cells to see the cell's approximate coordinates and exact annual average temperature.

Development Process

We developed the application using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and D3.js. The application is divided into several files, with HTML providing the page structure, CSS controlling the layout and appearance, and D3.js handling the map rendering, data visualization, and interactions.

The data preparation process was implemented separately in Python.
